\begin{abstract}
Safety-aligned language models frequently exhibit severe calibration failures in low-resource scripts, either refusing benign queries that contain sensitive clinical, legal, or security terms (over-refusal) or complying with harmful instructions when transliterated into Romanized scripts (under-refusal). We investigate the geometric mechanism behind this failure across English (\texttt{en}), native Bangla (\texttt{bn}), and Romanized Bangla (\texttt{bn\_latn}) using \textbf{X-ORSC-Bench}, a controlled parallel benchmark of 360 prompts spanning 40 scenarios across four sensitive domains and three intent levels. First, we quantify byte-pair encoding fragmentation across \texttt{Qwen2.5} and \texttt{Qwen3.5}, finding that native Bangla suffers a $6.27\times$ subword fertility penalty relative to English in \texttt{Qwen2.5} ($7.71 \pm 0.82$ vs.\ $1.23 \pm 0.14$ tokens/word) and a $100.00\%$ morpheme fragmentation rate. Second, probing hidden states across all 24 transformer layers reveals \textbf{Cross-Lingual Axis Collapse}: whereas English maintains separated subspaces between the over-refusal axis and the genuine safe-completion axis in mid-to-late layers ($\text{CLACI} = 0.91 \pm 0.19$), subword fragmentation collapses these axes in native Bangla ($\text{CLACI} = 0.98 \pm 0.00$) and Romanized Bangla ($\text{CLACI} = 0.98 \pm 0.00$). Third, we propose \textbf{Cross-Lingual Orthogonal Safe-Completion Steering (\texttt{CL-OSCS})}, which removes script-centroid shift, projects the safe-completion direction orthogonally to the English refusal subspace, and scales interventions by an inverse-fertility factor. Under 5-fold scenario cross-validation, \texttt{CL-OSCS} increases Net Safe-Completion Score (\texttt{NSCS}) from $2.22\%$ to $63.89\%$ on \texttt{Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct} and from $1.94\%$ to $50.00\%$ on \texttt{Qwen3.5-0.8B}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Safety alignment in multilingual large language models aims to balance helpfulness on benign inquiries with harmlessness on dangerous requests and honesty regarding model boundaries \cite{ouyang2022training,bai2022constitutional}. In practice, this calibration breaks down outside high-resource Latin-script languages \cite{wang2024all,qin2024multilingual}. Users querying models in low-resource scripts encounter two opposing failure modes: inflated false-positive refusal rates on benign boundary questions (\textbf{over-refusal}) \cite{rottger2024xstest,cui2024orbench}, and unsafe compliance when harmful prompts are written in low-resource or Romanized scripts (\textbf{under-refusal}) \cite{deng2024multilingual,yong2023lowresource}.

We hypothesize that subword over-segmentation in byte-pair encoding (BPE) tokenizers induces geometric interference between the over-refusal axis $\mathbf{v}_{\text{over}}$ and the safe-completion axis $\mathbf{v}_{\text{safe}}$ in mid-to-late transformer layers, a phenomenon we term \textbf{Cross-Lingual Axis Collapse}. To test and resolve this failure mode, we introduce \textbf{X-ORSC-Bench} (360 parallel prompts across \texttt{en}, \texttt{bn}, and \texttt{bn\_latn}) and formulate \textbf{Cross-Lingual Orthogonal Safe-Completion Steering (\texttt{CL-OSCS})}.

\section{Mechanistic Subspace Geometry and \texttt{CL-OSCS}}
Within scenario $i$ and script $s$, we define the over-refusal shift $\mathbf{d}_{\text{over},i,s}^{(\ell)} = \mathbf{h}_{i,s,\text{bb}}^{(\ell)} - \mathbf{h}_{i,s,\text{bg}}^{(\ell)}$ and the safe-completion shift $\mathbf{d}_{\text{safe},i,s}^{(\ell)} = \mathbf{h}_{i,s,\text{bb}}^{(\ell)} - \mathbf{h}_{i,s,\text{harm}}^{(\ell)}$. Across mid-to-late layers $\mathcal{L}_{\text{mid-late}} = \{14,\dots,22\}$, the Cross-Lingual Axis Collapse Index is:
\begin{equation}
\text{CLACI}_{i,s} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{L}_{\text{mid-late}}|} \sum_{\ell \in \mathcal{L}_{\text{mid-late}}} \left| \cos\big(\mathbf{d}_{\text{over},i,s}^{(\ell)}, \mathbf{d}_{\text{safe},i,s}^{(\ell)}\big) \right|
\end{equation}
\texttt{CL-OSCS} applies script-centroid mean-centering $\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{i,s}^{(\ell)} = \mathbf{h}_{i,s}^{(\ell)} - \beta \boldsymbol{\delta}_s^{(\ell)}$, Gram-Schmidt orthogonal projection relative to the English refusal unit vector $\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{\text{ref,en}}^{(\ell)}$, and inverse-fertility scaling $\gamma_\tau(x) = \alpha (F_\tau(x)/\bar{F}_{\tau,\text{en}})^{-\eta}$.

\section{Conclusion}
Subword fragmentation in low-resource native and transliterated scripts drives Cross-Lingual Axis Collapse between over-refusal and safe-completion subspaces. \texttt{CL-OSCS} restores orthogonality and substantially improves cross-lingual safety calibration.
